A web simulator for the optical properties of solids — thin films, bulk crystals, and their response to magnetic field and temperature — intended for both teaching and research. Below, ● marks what is available and ○ what is planned.

  • Single-interface Fresnel coefficients r, t and phases arg r, arg t
  • Bulk reflectance vs. magnetic field and temperature (LaSb; Drude · Lorentz, ε±)
  • Reflectance and transmittance of multilayers
  • Ψ, Δ and tanΨ, tanΔ visualization
  • s-Brewster and special-angle search
  • Design tool for defect-absorption (ε₂) enhancement

About this site

This project grew out of research on enhancing and detecting very weak absorption signals in optical thin-film structures, and has since widened to the optical response of bulk materials under magnetic field and varying temperature. The aim is to move the dielectric-function modeling and transfer-matrix calculations used in the lab into a form anyone can explore with only a web browser.

The goal is to combine accuracy sufficient for research use with visualizations intuitive enough for teaching.

Developed by Hyun Don Kim, based on ongoing experimental work on thin-film optics and ellipsometry in the Department of Physics, University of Seoul.

Features (partly implemented)

1. Multilayer TMM simulator

Starting from air / film / substrate and extending to three or more layers with an inserted film, R and T are computed by the transfer matrix method and plotted.

2. Ellipsometric Ψ, Δ

Ψ, Δ and tanΨ will be derived from the s- and p-polarized reflection coefficients and presented in a format comparable to experimental data from V-VASE or CompleteEASE.

3. Defect-absorption enhancement design

A design tool in which a very weak Lorentz oscillator is added to ε₂, and the resulting enhancement of δΨ and δΔ can be examined while tuning the inserted-layer thickness and the angle of incidence.

4. Fresnel coefficients and phase (partly implemented)

The single-interface case is already available on the Fresnel page. The plan is to visualize |rp|, |rs| and the phases δp, δs at each interface against wavelength and angle of incidence, making their relation to the Brewster and s-Brewster special angles apparent.
